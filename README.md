Nest Egg is a household budget site for one family keeping its money straight. The dashboard shows what is left to spend in November 2026, with income, spending and savings at a glance, a donut of where the spending went — tap any slice or legend row and it opens Transactions filtered to that category — a month-pace strip (days left in the month, what is safe to spend each remaining day, and whether the flexible spending is ahead of or behind the plan — rent, which lands as one lump on the 1st, is left out of the pace so it never cries wolf), and a Next moves panel with the concrete steps the household's own numbers call for: covering an over-budget category from the budget with the most room, in one click, and the dollars needed to reach a 15% savings rate. Insights compares November with October, shows spending by week and by day, a savings-rate gauge against its 15% target, a ranked list of the biggest spending — and, since income used to show only as a total, a matching ranked list of where the money came from: salary carries the month at 76.8% of all income, with the other four sources beside it. The month also prints cleanly: navigation, buttons and forms drop out, and the dark hero prints as a plain bordered box instead of solid ink. Transactions can be added, edited inline, filtered and deleted, downloaded as a CSV of exactly the rows shown, and imported back from a CSV in the same shape; the list is grouped by day, newest first, with each day's own subtotal (income, spending and saving) so a day's money is visible without adding it up by hand. Nine monthly budgets show progress and can have their limits changed, each with a burn-rate line saying when its limit runs out at the current pace (or that a one-off payment is already made), and limits can be moved between budgets — the month's total never changes — with the form opening pre-filled with the best move; an all-budgets total sits at the top; three savings goals track progress and take top-ups, each with a projected finish date worked out from what the household actually saves into it each month, and a monthly plan saying what it would take each month to finish in 6 or 12 months, against the pace the household is actually on. Keyboard users get 1–5 to switch pages, / to jump to the transaction search, N to start a new transaction, and Esc to clear the search or close an open edit form, as well as a visible amber focus ring on every control, and status messages are announced to screen readers. Everything is static files — no build step, no network requests, no account — and changes are saved in the browser's local storage, with a one-click reset back to the demo month.
