Nest Egg is a household budget site for one family keeping its money straight. The dashboard shows what is left to spend in November 2026, with income, spending and savings at a glance, a donut of where the spending went — tap any slice or legend row and it opens Transactions filtered to that category — a month-pace strip (days left in the month, what is safe to spend each remaining day, and whether the flexible spending is ahead of or behind the plan — rent, which lands as one lump on the 1st, is left out of the pace so it never cries wolf), and a Next moves panel with the concrete steps the household's own numbers call for: covering an over-budget category from the budget with the most room, in one click, and the dollars needed to reach a 15% savings rate. Transactions can be added, edited inline, filtered and deleted, downloaded as a CSV of exactly the rows shown, and imported back from a CSV in the same shape; nine monthly budgets show progress and can have their limits changed, each with a burn-rate line saying when its limit runs out at the current pace (or that a one-off payment is already made), and limits can be moved between budgets — the month's total never changes — with the form opening pre-filled with the best move; an all-budgets total sits at the top; three savings goals track progress and take top-ups, each with a projected finish date worked out from what the household actually saves into it each month; Insights compares November with October, charts spending by week and by day (the biggest day was Nov 1, rent day, and 16 of the 30 days had no spending at all), shows the savings rate on a gauge against the 15% target, ranks the biggest spending, and turns the household's own numbers into plain-language tips. Adding or editing a transaction says what it does to that budget right away — over its limit, or nearly there — instead of leaving the household to notice on the Budgets page; searching transactions highlights the matching words in each row; and Insights offers the whole month as a plain-text report to download and keep. Keyboard users get a visible amber focus ring on every control, and status messages are announced to screen readers. Everything is static files — no build step, no network requests, no account — and changes are saved in the browser's local storage, with a one-click reset back to the demo month.
