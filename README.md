Nest Egg is a household budget site for one family keeping its money straight. The dashboard shows what is left to spend in November 2026, with income, spending and savings at a glance, a donut of where the spending went — tap any slice or legend row and it opens Transactions filtered to that category — a month-pace strip (days left in the month, what is safe to spend each remaining day, and whether the flexible spending is ahead of or behind the plan — rent, which lands as one lump on the 1st, is left out of the pace so it never cries wolf), the month's best and worst days by net money (payday, Sun Nov 1, took in $4,200.00 against $958.97 out for +$3,241.03; Thu Nov 5 quietly took the most out at −$420.00), and a Next moves panel with the concrete steps the household's own numbers call for: covering an over-budget category from the budget with the most room, in one click, and the dollars needed to reach a 15% savings rate. Insights compares November with October, shows spending by week and by day, a savings-rate gauge against its 15% target, a ranked list of the biggest spending — and, since income used to show only as a total, a matching ranked list of where the money came from: salary carries the month at 76.8% of all income, with the other four sources beside it — and, since the totals hid which categories actually moved, a table comparing November with October category by category: Shopping is up 59.1%, Entertainment down 32.5%. The month also prints cleanly: navigation, buttons and forms drop out, and the dark hero prints as a plain bordered box instead of solid ink. Transactions can be added, edited inline, filtered and deleted, downloaded as a CSV of exactly the rows shown, and imported back from a CSV in the same shape; the amount field has $5, $10, $20 and $50 quick taps for the amounts the household types most, and the note field offers the notes the household has already used for that category, newest first, so a weekly shop is one tap instead of retyping; the category filter only ever offers what the chosen type can actually have, so picking "spending" never shows "Salary" and switching types drops a category that could no longer match; the list is grouped by day, newest first, with each day's own subtotal (income, spending and saving) so a day's money is visible without adding it up by hand. Nine monthly budgets show progress and can have their limits changed, each with a burn-rate line saying when its limit runs out at the current pace (or that a one-off payment is already made), and limits can be moved between budgets — the month's total never changes — with the form opening pre-filled with the best move; an all-budgets total sits at the top; and the four regular bills that land every month — rent $920.00, the streaming bundle $38.97, electricity $132.45, internet and phone $89.99, $1,181.41 a month — are listed with whether each one has actually been paid, read from the ledger itself, so deleting the rent transaction flips it to "Not paid yet", with the next bill to pay named underneath; three savings goals track progress and take top-ups, each with a projected finish date worked out from what the household actually saves into it each month, milestone marks at 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% with the next one named and priced ("Next milestone: 75% — $540.00 more to go"), and a monthly plan saying what it would take each month to finish in 6 or 12 months, against the pace the household is actually on. Keyboard users get 1–5 to switch pages, / to jump to the transaction search, N to start a new transaction, and Esc to clear the search or close an open edit form, as well as a visible amber focus ring on every control, and status messages are announced to screen readers. The whole household can be backed up as one JSON file — every transaction, every limit and every goal — and restored from it later; a bad file is refused with the reason named and nothing changes, so a wrong file can never eat half a household. Importing a CSV never double-counts: a row already in the ledger — same date, kind, category, note and amount — is skipped and counted, so importing the file the site itself exported adds nothing, and the message says how many rows were already there. Resetting the demo data takes two clicks — the first arms it and says what the second will do, the arm wears off after a few seconds — so a stray click can never empty the ledger. And the site has a dark mode: a toggle in the top bar flips the whole household to a dark pine palette, remembers the choice on the device, and otherwise follows the device's own preference; printing still comes out light either way. Everything is static files — no build step, no network requests, no account — and changes are saved in the browser's local storage, with a two-click reset back to the demo month.
